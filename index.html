PROCEDURE

Three steps the first time.
One click every time after.

Paste a video link

YouTube, a direct video file, most video sites. Short loops — ten seconds to two minutes — make the best wallpapers.

Install once — about a minute

The first click offers you the app. It installs to your own user folder: no admin password, no system changes, and Windows Settings uninstalls it completely.

† Your browser will ask “Open Driftpaper?” — that's Windows handing your link to the app, not a warning. Tick always allow and it never asks again.

Your desktop comes alive

The video plays behind your icons. Change it any time by pasting a new link — Driftpaper waits in the tray with pause, scaling and startup options.

ii.

SPECIFICATIONS

A wallpaper runs all day. It has to behave.

inputYouTube & most video sites, or any direct video file — resolved by yt-dlp, played by mpv: open-source engines trusted by millions.
during gamesPlayback pauses automatically when any fullscreen app is in front. Zero frames stolen; your GPU belongs to the game.
on batteryPauses unplugged, resumes on AC. Both auto-pauses can be toggled from the tray.
renderingHardware video decode — the same silicon that plays YouTube. Fill, fit or stretch for ultrawide and portrait clips.
privacyNo account. No telemetry. No servers. Videos download from the source straight to your app-data folder and stay there.
soundAlways muted, by design. (That also makes downloads smaller — video stream only.)
updatesChecked at launch; a new version is one click away. Nothing installs behind your back.
footprint~300 MB on disk incl. its own player & runtime — nothing shared touched, nothing left after uninstall.
iii.

FAIR QUESTIONS

Asked bluntly, answered straight.

Is this safe to run?
The app can't touch system files even if it wanted to — it installs to your user folder and never asks for admin rights. Its only network activity: downloading the videos you request, and a version check at launch. The site and every release are public on GitHub.
Why does Windows warn me during install?
Because the beta isn't code-signed yet. Certificates prove a publisher's identity to Windows and cost real money — signing is planned before the public release. Until then, SmartScreen labels every unsigned program “unknown publisher”, ours included.
Will it eat my FPS?
No — that's the point of the fullscreen detector. A game takes the screen, playback stops within two seconds, and your GPU is untouched until you're back on the desktop.
Where do my videos end up?
In your local app-data folder, fetched by your own machine directly from the source site. There is no Driftpaper server to pass through — we couldn't see your videos if we tried.
And if I want it gone?
Windows Settings → Apps → Driftpaper → Uninstall. It stops playback, detaches from the desktop, and removes the app, cache and settings. Verified to leave zero traces.
